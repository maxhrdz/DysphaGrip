DysphaGrip.

Exploring swallowing-related events through wearable sensor fusion.

An experimental hardware-and-software system combining contact-microphone acoustics, motion sensing, and real-time processing to investigate signals associated with swallowing.

Proof of concept · Not intended for diagnosis or medical decision-making
01 / The motivation

Making swallowing research more accessible.

Dysphagia can affect people after a stroke or with other neurological conditions. Clinical assessment requires trained specialists and appropriate equipment. Our question was narrower: could a small set of low-cost sensors capture complementary signals that may be useful for researching swallow-event detection?

CHALLENGE 01

Complex biological signals

Swallowing involves subtle movement and acoustic activity that can vary between people and conditions.

CHALLENGE 02

Confounding actions

Speech, coughs, and ordinary head movement can resemble some sensor features of swallowing.

CHALLENGE 03

Experimental reliability

Calibration, sensor placement, and timing alignment matter before any useful evaluation is possible.

02 / Engineering

Two sensing paths. One experimental detector.

DysphaGrip acquires the microphone and motion sensor data through separate connections to a computer. Python processes the streams and records a candidate when calibrated features exceed their thresholds within a short coincidence window.

04 / What's next

From hackathon prototype to research questions.

Built at SanD Hacks 2026, DysphaGrip demonstrates an end-to-end sensing pipeline. Future work could explore carefully labeled datasets, robust signal-quality analysis, cross-user evaluation, and machine-learning baselines under appropriate research supervision.

Medical and ethical limitation. This system does not diagnose dysphagia, assess swallow safety, or detect or rule out aspiration (including silent aspiration). It is not approved or validated for clinical use. Do not test swallowing in people at risk without appropriate clinical oversight.
